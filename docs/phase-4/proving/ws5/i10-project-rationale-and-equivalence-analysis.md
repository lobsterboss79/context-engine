## Conclusion

The approved record expressly establishes **that** Company AI Roadmap is the first external proving ground, but does not record why that particular Project was preferred over identified alternatives, a domain-specific property, an employment/company relationship, or a permanent-selection rationale.  Its recorded role is to supply the external, fresh-Consumer proving case complementary to Context Engine dogfooding and to exercise meaningful continuation with less manual reconstruction.

The substantive proving properties are sufficiently stated that, in principle, an Owner-selected qualifying real external Project could preserve the general external-project capability claim.  That conclusion is an equivalence analysis only: the current baseline nevertheless requires Company AI Roadmap, so no substitute is permitted absent the separate governed multi-baseline amendment identified in the named-project trace.

## 3. Domain-specificity and work-project assessment

I10 requires an **AI Roadmap** package by name in the current baseline, but its articulated acceptance conditions are governance/context properties: identity, purpose, state, authority, authorization, current work, legitimate next activity, constraints, unresolved matters, Source state, Provenance, state distinctions, isolation, adversarial evidence handling, and reconstruction burden.  No record requires an AI-roadmap domain, business/work domain, or company-operations domain as an independent proving property.

A documentation/governance-rich Project is **strongly implied** to be useful because I10 must exercise the listed governed information; however, no approved taxonomy says a Project must be “documentation-heavy” or sets a minimum artifact count.  The measurable question is whether its actual authorized task-relative Sources can establish the required claims and limitations.

Likewise, **real Project** is distinct from **work Project**.  The former is material; the latter is not established as a requirement.  A personal Project could be eligible in principle under a future amendment if it meets the same authority, Source, task, freshness, disclosure, control, and evidence conditions.  This conclusion does not infer any employer/compliance requirement; project-specific authorization remains a separate factual prerequisite for any selected Project.

## 5. What replacement cannot preserve, and what is not shown to be unique

A replacement cannot preserve the literal historical claim that the Consumer continued **Company AI Roadmap**, its particular task, Source identities, terminology, Source topology, history, actual disclosure constraints, or its concrete reconstruction burden.  Those facts belong to the named unexecuted exercise and would require new run-specific evidence.

No approved record establishes that any of those particular facts is uniquely necessary to the general I10 external-project capability claim.  In particular, no recorded rationale makes Company AI Roadmap’s AI/business/work domain, employer affiliation, special compliance constraints, or a named Source topology irreplaceable.  A replacement must not claim equivalence of those facts; it must instead demonstrate equivalence against the standard in section 4.

## 6. Governance options (analysis only)

### Option A — retain original

Keep Company AI Roadmap as the required I10/WS6A Project.  This preserves the exact approved claim, original named target, and all historical traceability without amendment.  Its proving strength is maximal relative to the current baseline.

Its execution prerequisites remain substantial but already known: authorized access to the actual Project/Bootstrap/Sources, bounded Source/ASU and disclosure basis, frozen task/package/rendering/criteria, fair control baseline, distinct fresh control Consumer, raw evidence retention, independent evaluation, and separate WS6A authorization.  The existing external-project execution analysis’s company/work authorization statements are conditional execution facts for that target, not Context Engine governance requirements.  The disadvantages are those practical dependencies; no proving weakness is established.

### Option B — project-neutral amendment

Replace the named target with one Owner-selected qualifying real external Project under a frozen equivalence checklist.  If every mandatory section 4 criterion is met, this preserves the **substantive external-project capability** claim and its proving rigor, but changes the literal named-Company-AI-Roadmap claim.  Benefits are Project-selection flexibility and removal of target-specific practical constraints; risks are selecting a too-simple, synthetic, pre-briefed, disclosure-ineligible, or unfair-control Project and thereby weakening the claim.  The amendment must prohibit that outcome through the equivalence standard and separate selection.

This requires the multi-baseline amendment surface in section 7 and a new successor protocol.  It is not a current permission to select or run a Project.

### Option C — named replacement Project

Amend the baseline to name one different real Project.  Its potential proving strength equals Option B only if that Project demonstrably meets section 4; it additionally restores a concrete, auditable named target.  It has the benefit of unambiguous claim/task lineage and the drawback of prematurely binding the baseline to another Project before Source/ASU/control feasibility is frozen.  It combines the baseline amendment and Project-selection decision that Option B deliberately separates.  It must not name a Project until the Owner makes that distinct decision.
